## 2. AI-Fabricated Evidence Crisis

### The Problem (2024–2026)

AI-generated deepfakes, fabricated messages, and forged documents are being submitted as evidence in custody disputes:

- Judges have acknowledged deepfakes in family court, including fabricated audio recordings of partners making threats
- Forensically confirmed AI-manipulated recordings have been identified in custody proceedings
- Fabricated evidence includes deepfake videos showing unsafe living conditions or inappropriate behavior

### Regulatory Response

| Development | Timeline |
|------------|----------|
| Judicial Council review of AI's impact on evidence introduction | Directed by January 1, 2026 |
| Proposed Rule 901(c) addressing AI-generated evidence | On Advisory Committee agenda, fall 2025 |
| Proposed Federal Rule of Evidence 707 (machine-generated evidence) | Public comment ended February 16, 2026; expected effective December 2026 |

### Detection Standards

Courts increasingly require rigorous authentication: device identifiers, GPS coordinates, timestamps, cryptographic sealing at creation, and auditable chain of custody from capture to courtroom.

### Nookbud Advantage

This is Nookbud's strongest market timing argument. SHA-256 hashing + immutable version history + hash manifest export directly addresses the court's emerging need for trustworthy digital evidence.

## 6. Digital Evidence & Court Technology

### Digital Evidence Standards

Courts require authentication (Rule 901), integrity (no alteration), and chain of custody. Methods include cryptographic hash functions, digital signatures, and audit trails.

**Amendment to Rule 902 (2024):** Machine-generated data and forensic electronic evidence are now self-authenticating — no live witness required. This directly benefits platforms like Nookbud.

### Blockchain & Cryptographic Verification

- Federal Rules of Evidence 902(13)–(14) permit self-authentication from reliable systems
- Blockchain meets requirements through certified hashes and timestamping
- Technical standards (ETSI EN 319 401, ISO 23257:2022) establish qualifying frameworks
- Key limitation: blockchain proves integrity but not truthfulness of underlying content

### Court-Ordered Co-Parenting Apps

- OFW ordered by judges in all 50 US states, Canada, UK, NZ, Australia
- AppClose extended LA County Superior Court agreement for 3.5 years (2026)
- DC courts formally approved multiple co-parenting platforms (2026)
- Court adoption is incremental: attorneys recommend → judges incorporate → jurisdictions normalize
