The product is the well-being of the child. Co-parenting is one way adults share that week. It is not the name of the app.

## The story

A child has a week. Someone has to know where they need to be, what is still undone, what money was for them, and what they hope to be given. Kids love gifts. The list is how the adults share that, so two people do not buy the same thing and a parent far away can see what the child asked for.

More than one adult usually holds that week. Two parents in one home. A parent in another house. A grandparent. A guardian. A parent working abroad who still has a say. A lawyer, when the family invites them to read the same facts.

Nookbud is the shared place for that week. The child has no account. The adults do.

What the adults keep:

1. A date. Where the child needs to be, and who takes them.
2. A task. A form, a supply, a fee, a thing to pack. One adult owns it. When it is done, the next list leaves it out.
3. Money for the child. Sent, spent, or still due, and what it was for. The app does not move the money.
4. A wish. Something the child hopes to be given. An adult writes it down. The child has no account and does not shop in the app.

If a day has nothing due, the place stays quiet. If a deadline sits inside seven days, one line is enough.

That loop is already how a family week gets run by hand: mail and letters in, a calendar and a task list out, payments called out and never made by the tool, a note to the other adult kept as a draft until a person sends it. Nookbud is that loop in one place the invited adults can see.

## Who it is for

In this order. Each one is a way to use a space. A space is still the private circle around a child. An invitation is an email. Nothing checks that the person is a parent or a relative.

| Adult | What they are here for |
| --- | --- |
| Parents in one home | School, health, and the things the child needs, on one list both of them see. |
| A parent in another home | Whose day it is, and a request to change a day. A use of the app, not the headline. |
| Grandparent or guardian | The adult with the child. Daily care, school, clinic. |
| A parent abroad | Sees the same week. Records money sent and what it was for. Picks from the child's list. Keeps a say. |
| A lawyer the family invites | Reads or exports the week. Does not run the morning. |

A lawyer is in the story in two narrow ways. The family can invite them to read dates, finished tasks, money, and agreements. Or they are already part of the child's care, for example on a guardianship or a support matter, and the family adds them to the space. The app does not give legal advice, file a case, or replace a power of attorney.

## How the same week looks

These are composite scenes for the story. They are not customers, and they are not one real family.

**One home.** School sends a program and a fee. One parent puts the morning on the calendar, the form on the list, and the fee as money still due. The other parent sees it before the bag is packed. When the fee is paid, it leaves the list.

**Parent abroad, guardian here.** The parent sends money for school and medicine. The grandparent marks what it paid for, adds the school day, and notes the clinic. The parent abroad sees that week without hunting through a chat. Being abroad does not end their say. The grandparent's daily care is written down.

**Two homes.** Whose day, and a request to swap a day, stay. The sample week is a school pickup, a dentist visit, and boots. It is not a handoff painted in two colors.

**Gifts.** The child names a few things for a birthday or a holiday. An adult writes them on one list anyone in the space can see. Someone marks an item promised, then bought. The money record says it was for that wish. A parent abroad and a grandparent are not guessing, and they are not buying two of the same toy. The child never signs in.

**A lawyer.** They open the same week the family already keeps. They can export it. They do not pack the bag, and they do not run the gift list.

## What the records can hold today

A record is still only a note, an agreement, an expense, or an event. This story does not add a type.

| Part of the week | Held today as | Later, only if asked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the child needs to be | Event | — |
| Who is doing the day, and a swap | Schedule and change requests, as now | — |
| A form, a supply, a thing to pack | Note | A task with an owner, a due day, and a done mark |
| Money sent, spent, or still due | Expense | Those three states, tied to the child |
| What the child hopes to be given | Note | A wish on a shared list: wanted, promised, bought, or given. Buying it can point at the expense. |
| A decision both adults accept | Agreement | — |
| A power of attorney or a court paper | Not in this plan | A file on the space |

There is no digest in the app. The feeling to protect is a short list of what is still open, not a feed of everything that ever happened.

Nookbud does not connect to a remittance app, a bank, a shop, or a school portal. Those systems move money, sell a gift, or hold grades. This one says what the money was for, whether the child's week was covered, and which wish was met.

## What the research supports

Figures below are for the story. They are not a market-size claim for a price.

**The money is for the child's ordinary needs.** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, Consumer Expectations Survey, 3rd quarter 2025: of 366 households that received remittances, 95.4% used some for food and other household needs, 72.7% for education, and 62.0% for medical expenses. A household can name more than one use, so the shares do not add to 100. Savings were 39.3%. Debt payments were 18.3%. Motor vehicles were 7.7%. Source: [CES 3q 2025 (PDF)](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Lists/Consumer%20Expectation%20Report/Attachments/24/CES_3qtr2025.pdf).

**The sums are large. The worker count is not "10 million OFWs."** The BSP cash-remittance table shows 2025 as preliminary: $35.63 billion (35,633,784 thousand US dollars). Land-based $28.49 billion, sea-based $7.14 billion. Source: [BSP overseas Filipino cash remittances](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/statistics/external/ofw2.aspx). The Philippine Statistics Authority estimated 2.19 million OFWs in 2024, up 1.5% from 2.16 million, for workers who worked abroad in the survey window. Women were 57.2%. Asia was 74.5% of destinations. Source: [PSA SOF 2024 special release (PDF)](https://psa.gov.ph/system/files/iesd/SOF-2024-Special-Release.pdf). That is not a count of all Filipinos abroad, and it is not a count of children. The old discovery line "10M+ OFWs" should not be reused.

**A parent abroad still has authority. The adult with the child still needs a way to act.** Family Code article 214: on death, absence, or unsuitability of the parents, the surviving grandparent exercises substitute parental authority. Article 216, when there is no parent and no court-appointed guardian, in order: the surviving grandparent, then the oldest sibling over 21, then the child's actual custodian over 21. Statute text: [Legal Resource PH, Title IX](https://library.legalresource.ph/title-ix-parental-authority-family-code/).

Working abroad is not, by itself, the "absence" that strips that authority. GMA reported a July 2024 Supreme Court Second Division decision, penned by Justice Jhosep Lopez and published 31 January 2025: an OFW mother kept parental authority, and the children stayed in the grandmother's provisional care. The father had argued she was absent because she worked in France. Quote as reported: "The mere fact that a parent is an OFW does not deprive them of their right to exercise parental authority or sole custody." Source: [GMA News, 31 Jan 2025](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/pinoyabroad/dispatch/934746/ofws-do-not-lose-custody-rights-parental-authority-over-children-supreme-court/story/). The decision text and the G.R. number were not opened on the Supreme Court site.

Day to day, families commonly use a written authorization or a special power of attorney so the adult in the Philippines can deal with school and clinic while the parent abroad still decides. That paper is not a court guardianship. A practitioner explainer, not a statute: [Respicio & Co. on grandparent care for an OFW parent's child](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/grandparent-guardianship-of-illegitimate-child-for-ofw-parent-in-the-philippines).

No current official count of children living with grandparents or guardians was found. The story does not use one.

## What the old story got wrong

The discovery brief already said the product was care across family shapes, then led with "Two homes. One childhood." The page title, sign-up, and welcome still use that line. The landing page says "across homes" and "both homes." The sample week is a handoff in two colors.

The ideal-customer file leads with a separated parent in Atlanta, then an OFW, a grandparent, a US adoption, and a blended family in Melbourne. The Atlanta lead, and the "10M+ OFWs" line, fight this story. `docs/discovery/` and `docs/research/` stay as old reference. Do not rewrite them in this plan. Do not quote them on the site.

Court-ready records stay underneath. Most families never need a lawyer. The week has to be useful on a Tuesday morning or the record never gets written.
